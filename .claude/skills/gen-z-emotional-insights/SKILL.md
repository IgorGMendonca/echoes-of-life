# Audience and emotional insight
Audience: international English-speaking teens and young adults, roughly 16–25; never assume all share the same circumstances. Write about experiences, not diagnoses.

## Topic map
- Social comparison: highlight reels versus everyday life; milestones on different schedules.
- Future uncertainty: not knowing what to study, career decisions, changing goals.
- Time anxiety: fear of wasting youth, pressure to optimize every moment, ordinary days.
- Nostalgia: childhood routines, old friends, moving away, changing family traditions.
- Identity and belonging: changing friendships, trying to fit in, feeling unseen.
- Rest and growth: small progress, flexibility, permission to rest and ask for help.

## Responsible writing
Validate feelings without treating fears as facts. Avoid countdowns to failure, guilt, shame, fear of missing out as a pressure tactic, glamorizing distress, diagnoses, or suggesting a person's value depends on achievement. Never target or personalize by mental-health vulnerability. Do not promise a cure or use false neuroscience. Avoid framing normal uncertainty as pathology. If discussing serious distress, keep it supportive and age-appropriate, encourage trusted support when relevant.

For psychology claims: verify using reputable research or institutional sources, store links and claims in a source note, distinguish evidence from interpretation, and qualify uncertainty. If research cannot be accessed, omit factual claims or frame the piece as a personal reflection. Use no invented statistics or sources.

Deliver for each idea: lived moment, feeling, compassionate reframing, format, and evidence note if applicable. Vary topics and settings across a batch.
