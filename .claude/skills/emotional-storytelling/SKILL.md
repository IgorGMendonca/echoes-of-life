# Emotional storytelling
Write for an international audience aged approximately 16–25. Be empathetic, concrete and understated. Speak as a thoughtful friend, not a therapist or motivational guru.

## Narrative workflow
1. Choose one emotional tension and one specific everyday moment (a graduation photo, unread message, late-night commute, an old bedroom).
2. Create 3 distinct opening lines. Choose the one that is honest, specific and immediately intelligible.
3. Build a progression: recognition, concrete detail, reframing, quiet resolution. For narrative stories, use character, situation, change and ending. Label invented stories as fiction in the publication metadata; never pass them off as real.
4. Use short natural spoken sentences, contractions and sensory details. Read aloud; avoid generic motivational platitudes, repetitive rhetorical questions and exaggerated sadness.
5. For 7–15 second aesthetic edits use one idea in roughly 8–22 words, checked against actual on-screen reading time. For 65–90 second narrated videos start with roughly 130–190 words and adjust to actual voice duration; do not pad to reach a minute.
6. End with agency or perspective rather than guilt, urgency or a demand to comment. No engagement bait.

## Examples of direction (do not repeat verbatim)
- Feeling behind: a friend announces a new job while you are still deciding what to study.
- Growing up: the familiar route home changes before you notice.
- Time: an ordinary evening that later becomes a cherished memory.

Deliver: theme, emotional arc, 3 hooks, chosen script, timed scene plan, post caption, optional genuine question. All public-facing text in English. Coordinate with gen-z-emotional-insights, cinematic-video-direction and video-quality-review.
